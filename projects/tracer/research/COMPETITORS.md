# Tracer — Competitor Audit

**Date:** 2026-06-22
**Scope:** 7 landings — direct rivals (Loom, Cap), premium-Mac adjacents (Screen Studio, CleanShot X, Tella), and the OSS/no-account fringe (OpenLoom, Screenity). Structural notes for section composition.

**Headline finding — the color gap:** every meaningful competitor sits in **blue/teal** (Loom, Cap, CleanShot #0669FF) or **purple/violet** (Tella). **Record-red (#E5484D) is unclaimed in this category.** Tracer can own a color the whole market avoids — and red literally *is* the record button. Free differentiation.

**Second finding — the positioning gap:** Cap already owns "own your recordings," but with **developer infrastructure** (S3 bucket / Google Drive) and a **soft, GDPR-corporate tone** ("No more being locked into proprietary platforms"). Tracer's wedge: ownership stated in a language a *non-developer already understands* (**Dropbox**), with a **sharper emotional frame** ("stop renting") instead of polite compliance-speak.

---

### 1. Loom — loom.com
**Positioning:** the async-video category king; "record and share AI-powered video messages."
**Sections (top→bottom):** nav → hero (tagline + embedded recording examples) → 24-logo social-proof wall (HubSpot, Tesla, Disney) → AI bug-report feature → recording → editing → integrations → collaboration → security → use-case cards (Sales/Eng/Support/Design) → feature list → blog → enterprise → testimonials → CTA footer. ~14 sections.
**Hero approach:** "One video is worth a thousand words." Embedded fake-recording player tiles ("1:1 with Dereje", "Quarterly Budget Meeting") with playback chrome.
**Accent + typography:** blue/teal CTAs; clean modern sans, hierarchical.
**Motion:** standard SaaS reveals; product video tiles.
**What works:** instant credibility via logo wall; the player tiles make the output legible at a glance.
**What doesn't:** maximalist 14-section sprawl = AI-slop sprawl by our doctrine; no pricing on page (you find the wall *after* you're hooked); zero ownership story — the exact flank we attack.
**What to steal:** the *legible-output* idea (show the actual share artifact), NOT the structure. Invert the funnel: lead with what Loom hides (you own the file, it's free).

### 2. Cap — cap.so  *(the direct threat — study hardest)*
**Positioning:** "Record and share in seconds." / "Beautiful screen recordings, owned by you." Open-source Loom alternative.
**Sections:** nav → hero → "three modes in one app" → "Truly Open Source" → "Privacy by Default, Sharing by Choice" → features → testimonials → pricing → FAQ → Loom-migration tools.
**Hero approach:** speed-first ("in seconds"); product modes shown as app UI.
**Ownership framing:** *"Fully open source, and you can connect your own Google Drive or S3 bucket, so you own every recording."* Comparison page: "No more being locked into proprietary platforms", "100% ownership and control", "platform dependent" (Loom) vs "100% with own storage" (Cap).
**Accent + typography:** blue/teal; clean sans.
**Loom strategy:** *soft* — "Coming from Loom? Bring your library with you" + importer tools. Never attacks.
**What works:** the ownership table is genuinely persuasive; migration tools lower switching cost; OSS-as-trust.
**What doesn't / what to beat:**
  - **Storage = S3/Drive** → speaks to devs only. **Tracer's Dropbox = ownership the designer/PM/founder already gets.** Wider, warmer.
  - **Tone is polite-corporate** ("Privacy by Default") → forgettable. **Tracer's "stop renting" is a verb, a grievance, a side to take.**
  - **Color is the same blue as Loom** → blends in. Tracer's red separates on sight.
  - Cap has a paid Pro tier; **Tracer is free forever** — say it louder than they can.

### 3. Screen Studio — screen.studio
**Positioning:** "the screen recorder that makes your videos look cinematic" — auto-zoom, cursor smoothing, device frames, macOS-only, indie (Adam Pietrasiak). Subscription: $29/mo or $108/yr.
**Hero approach:** the *output* is the hero — a buttery auto-zoomed recording playing in a device frame, motion doing the selling. Zero-timeline "it's already beautiful" promise.
**Accent + typography:** restrained, premium, near-monochrome with tasteful product video.
**Motion:** the cinematic auto-zoom *is* the demo; the page feels like the product.
**What works (steal this):** **the landing feels like the app.** Premium = the product literally performing on screen, smooth motion, device frame, no chrome clutter. This is the bar for Tracer's "MAX WOW cinematic hero."
**What doesn't:** pure subscription, no ownership/free story — different buyer (creators). Not our positioning, but **our craft benchmark.**
**What to steal:** the discipline of *showing, not listing*; the device-frame product hero; motion that demonstrates instead of decorates.

### 4. CleanShot X — cleanshot.com
**Positioning:** "Capture your Mac's screen like a pro." One-time license, premium native Mac tool.
**Sections:** hero → 30-day-guarantee + testimonial → feature overview → deep feature sections (Quick Access, Annotate, Recording) → premium add-ons (Cloud, Scrolling) → testimonials (named tech figures) → footer.
**Accent + typography:** clean blue #0669FF used *sparingly*; black-on-white high contrast; modern sans; minimal ornament.
**What works:** **native-Mac restraint** — feels like a macOS app, real product screenshots not abstract illustration, "completely native / super fast on-device" copy. Lightweight, intentional.
**What doesn't:** still a feature-list march once you scroll; cloud is *their* cloud (the lock-in we avoid).
**What to steal:** the macOS-native visual register; real product imagery over illustration; sparing accent; the ~12MB/native/fast credibility cues (Tracer's ~12MB is a hero-grade fact here).

### 5. Tella — tella.com
**Positioning:** "all-in-one screen recorder. Record, edit and share every video your business needs." Team-updates + YouTube creators.
**Hero approach:** dual-use headline ("Team updates in the morning / YouTube videos in the afternoon") + interactive editor demo (Cut/Layout/Zoom/Effects/Subs).
**Accent + typography:** **vibrant purple/violet gradient** + clean sans.
**Motion:** smooth editor-state transitions, testimonial carousel.
**What works:** "More videos. Less subscriptions" anti-subscription jab; transcript-era / text-based-editing angle.
**What doesn't (avoid):** **purple gradient = generic 2023 SaaS** — the exact look our doctrine bans and our red replaces; all-in-one editor scope is bloat for Tracer's "hit record, get a link" simplicity.
**What to steal:** only the *anti-subscription* sentiment, reframed as ownership not price.

### 6. OpenLoom — github.com/anenthg/OpenLoom
**Positioning:** "Open-source Loom alternative you self-host. Privacy-first." Recordings on your own Supabase. "no third-party servers, no vendor lock-in… no analytics, no telemetry, no account required."
**What works (steal the *copy posture*):** the bluntness — "no analytics, no telemetry, no account required" is exactly Tracer's register. The no-account-to-watch promise is a real shared value.
**What doesn't:** README-as-landing, self-host friction (Supabase setup) → too technical for Tracer's wider audience. Tracer wins by making the same values *one-click* (Dropbox, not "spin up Supabase").

### 7. Screenity — (Chrome extension, OSS)
**Positioning:** account-free screen+webcam recording, instant share link, self-host, "complete control over your data."
**What works:** proves "no account + instant link + your data" is a wanted bundle.
**What doesn't:** browser extension, not a native ~12MB Mac app; weaker on polish. Tracer = same freedom, native-app craft.

## Synthesis

**What most of them do that we should copy:**
- **Show the output, not a feature list** (Loom tiles, Screen Studio device frame). The single most persuasive move in the category.
- **Native-Mac restraint** (CleanShot, Screen Studio): real product imagery, sparing accent, the page feels like the app.
- **Ownership as the wedge against Loom** (Cap, OpenLoom): it's a proven, resonant attack vector — we just do it sharper and to a wider audience.

**What all of them do that's boring — skip it:**
- **The 10–14-section SaaS march** (Loom, Tella, CleanShot tail): logo wall → feature → feature → use-case cards → testimonials → enterprise. Doctrine bans this sprawl. Tracer is short, dense, confident.
- **Blue or purple accent** — the entire category. Record-red is our flag.
- **"AI-powered" as a banner headline** (Loom). AI titles/transcripts are quiet features for us, never the lead.
- **Soft, polite anti-lock-in language** (Cap's "Privacy by Default"). Forgettable. We say "stop renting."
- **Their cloud dressed up as ownership** (Loom/CleanShot Cloud). The lock-in we exist to refuse.

**The single defensible position no competitor occupies:** *the ownership story told in a tool civilians already trust (Dropbox), in red, free forever, with a sharper grievance ("stop renting") than the one OSS rival who shares the thesis.*
